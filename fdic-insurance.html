FDIC Coverage

How the $250,000 limit actually works, which structures reach $1,250,000, and the FDIC record for every bank reviewed.

The requirement was "max FDIC insurance". That is set at $250,000 per depositor, per FDIC-insured bank, per ownership category, and it is automatic once the money sits in a deposit account at an FDIC-insured bank. What changes is how many ownership categories you use.

Per depositor, per bank$250,000 Single-owner savings account
Joint account, 2 owners$500,000 $250,000 per co-owner
POD with 5 beneficiaries$1,250,000 Capped at $1,250,000 per owner
Five separate banks$1,250,000 $250,000 single-owner each

Single-owner account with 3 POD beneficiaries, one bank1 owner x 3 beneficiaries x $250,000$750,000FDIC
Single-owner account with 5 POD beneficiaries, one bank1 owner x 5 beneficiaries x $250,000 (equals the $1,250,000 per-owner trust cap)$1,250,000FDIC
Single-owner accounts at 5 different FDIC-insured banks5 banks x $250,000$1,250,000FDIC

The trust formula is quoted directly from the FDIC: "# of Owners × # of Beneficiaries × $250,000 = Amount Insured (not to exceed $1,250,000 per owner for all trust accounts)". Whether a specific bank will let you name five POD beneficiaries on its savings account is a bank-level question and is not assumed here.

Why splitting across five banks is harder than it looks

  • Interest drifts you over the limit. Coverage is principal plus interest through the date of failure, so a balance parked at exactly $250,000 will grow past the cap. Leave headroom or sweep interest out.
  • Branches do not multiply coverage. The FDIC is explicit: "Deposits in separate branches of an insured bank are not separately insured." Five branches of one bank is still one $250,000 limit. Source: FDIC — When a Bank Fails.
  • POD accounts need real beneficiary data. Ally's FDIC page notes that naming beneficiaries requires identifying information (address, date of birth, government ID number) for each one, to satisfy the FDIC's recordkeeping rules. Source: Ally — FDIC Insurance.
  • Brands are not banks. Capital One's help centre states Discover merged into Capital One on 2025-05-18 and that from 2025-11-18 Capital One and Discover deposit accounts are jointly insured, with some Discover CDs separately insured until maturity. The FDIC record for Discover Bank shows it as inactive. Two logos can be one $250,000 limit. Source: Capital One — Discover FAQs.
  • Network programs have their own terms. SoFi advertises coverage up to $3M "through a network of participating banks". That is a sweep arrangement, not a single-bank limit, and it carries program risk that a POD account at one bank does not.
  • Ownership category is the real lever. A single-owner savings account, an IRA, a joint account and a POD account at the same bank are separate categories — they can stack at one bank.

What FDIC insurance does and does not cover

Covered

  • Checking accounts
  • Savings accounts
  • Money Market Deposit Accounts (MMDAs)
  • Certificates of Deposit (CDs)

Not covered

  • Mutual funds
  • Annuities
  • Life insurance policies
  • Stocks and bonds
  • Crypto assets
  • Municipal securities
  • Safe deposit contents

Verify any specific balance with the FDIC's own calculator, EDIE, and confirm the institution in BankFind.
