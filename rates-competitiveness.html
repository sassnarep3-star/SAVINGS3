How to read the life of the deposit

Why a 1-year view flatters branch banks

A 0.01% and a 3.55% APY differ by $3.54 per $1,000 per year. Held five years the gap compounds: $1,000 grows to $1,000.05 at 0.01% but $1,190.34 at 3.55% — a $190 difference on a $1,000 deposit. The longer the horizon, the more the rate matters and the less the sign-up bonus does.

Why a 5-year view overstates a savings account

Every account in the returns tables is variable. None of these banks promises 3.55% for five years. Only a CD locks a rate, and the CD rates found on official pages are listed below so the trade-off is visible.

Break-even against a bonus: a $200 one-time bonus equals one year of the rate gap on about $6,309 at 3.55% versus 0.38%. Above that balance the APY dominates; below it, take the bonus.
