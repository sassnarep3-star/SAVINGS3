What the research found

  1. The gap is enormous. The best verified savings APY is 3.55% (Synchrony Bank). The weakest verified big-branch rate is 0.04% (Bank of America). On $250,000 that is $8,875 versus $100 a year — both fully FDIC-insured.
  2. Rate is not the only requirement. Chase, Citi and TD do not publish a readable savings APY without a ZIP code or a script, so their rates are left blank here rather than copied from a rate aggregator. They are still listed with their fees, minimums and rules.
  3. Standard branch-network rates are not built to hold large balances. Every non-promotional rate verified at a branch network on this site falls between 0.01% and 3.25% APY. At Bank of America's 0.04% a $1.25M balance earns $500 a year before fees. The same $1.25M at 3.55% earns $44,375, roughly 89× more.
  4. FDIC coverage is a design problem, not a bank problem. $250,000 per depositor per bank per ownership category, or up to $1,250,000 per owner through a payable-on-death structure with five beneficiaries. The coverage page prices out four structures.
  5. Promotions move the answer more than the APY does. Chase pays a $200 bonus on a $10,000 deposit held 90 days; SoFi and Wells Fargo run APY boosts. Those are priced on the bonuses page.
