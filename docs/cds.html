CDs — the only way to lock a rate

A CD’s APY is fixed to maturity if you hold it to the end — unlike savings APYs, which can drop at any time. In exchange, withdrawing principal early costs a penalty in days of interest (listed below). FDIC national averages (Oct 19, 2026): 12-month CD 1.76%, 24-month 1.55%, 36-month 1.39%, 60-month 1.41% (fdic.gov) — the best big-bank CDs below pay 2½–3× those averages.

Best verified CD offers: Marcus pays 4.40% APY on every term from 12 months through 6 years ($500 min). Ally’s 18-month is 4.40% with no minimum and the gentlest penalties. Capital One’s 11-month special and Amex’s 10- and 22-month terms also hit 4.40%. Branch CDs are a different world: Chase standard CDs pay 0.01% and most BofA Fixed Term CDs pay 0.03%.

CD vs. HYSA — which wins?

Today’s best CD (4.40% fixed) beats today’s best HYSA (3.50% variable) by 0.90 points — $900 more per $100,000 over one year if the HYSA rate never moved. The HYSA could rise above 4.40% (CD loses) or fall (CD wins more). The CD trades flexibility for certainty: breaking Ally’s 12-month CD early costs 60 days of interest (~$71 per $10,000 at 4.30%), while Amex’s 60-month penalty is 540 days (~$444 per $10,000 at 3.00%). A common middle path is a no-penalty CD (Marcus 11-month at 4.00%; Ally No Penalty at 2.80%) — fixed rate, principal accessible per its terms.
