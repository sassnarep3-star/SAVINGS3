Money market deposit accounts

A money market deposit account is an FDIC-insured bank account with savings-like interest plus check/debit-card access. (Do not confuse it with a money market fund — a mutual fund that is not FDIC-insured.) The FDIC money-market national average is 0.63% APY (Oct 19, 2026). Verified big-bank offers span 3.40% (conditional) down to 0.01%.

Which should you pick?

  • Want check/debit access and top pay with no hoops? Ally Money Market (3.10%, no fee, no minimum) — note the 10-withdrawals-per-cycle limit.
  • Already a U.S. Bank customer with $25k+ to park and brand-new to their money markets? Elite’s 3.40% is the category high — but it collapses to 0.01% the moment your daily balance dips below $25,000, and the $10 fee needs a $10,000+ balance to waive.
  • Truist One Money Market (0.01%) pays 63× less than the national money-market average and still risks a $12/month fee — for yield seekers, the math is unambiguous.

Marcus (3.50%), Amex (3.20%) and Capital One (3.20%) savings accounts all pay more than any verified money market except U.S. Bank’s conditional 3.40% — the only reason to choose a money market over them is check/debit access.
