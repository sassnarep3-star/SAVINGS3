High-yield savings accounts (HYSAs)

High-yield savings accounts are ordinary FDIC-insured savings accounts that pay a much higher variable APY than branch savings — 3.10%–3.50% verified here, vs. the FDIC national average of 0.38% (Oct 19, 2026). They are usually opened digitally and have no check-writing (that’s money market territory). All APYs below are variable — they can go up or down after opening. Only CD APYs are fixed for a term.

Our verified pick: Marcus Online Savings — 3.50% APY, no minimum, no fees, no linked product required, no balance tiers. If you want branches/cafés as well as online, Capital One (3.20%) is the only top-rate option here with physical locations. U.S. Bank and Wells Fargo reach similar highs only behind relationship/new-customer conditions — details below.

How competitive are these rates?

Marcus at 3.50% is 9.2× the FDIC national savings average (0.38%, Oct 19, 2026). Every unconditional HYSA above (Marcus, Amex, Capital One, Ally) is 8×+. The conditional offers (U.S. Bank, Wells Fargo) only beat them if you meet and keep meeting the balance/relationship rules — fall short by one day at Wells and you drop from 3.25% to 0.01% standard. Because these APYs are variable, today’s winner may not win in a year: see the Returns page for why 2–5 year “earnings” for HYSAs are hypothetical, and CDs if you want a rate that cannot fall.

Deposit size and insurance

Marcus allows up to $1,000,000 per account / $3,000,000 per account owner (Marcus FAQ) — but FDIC insurance still stops at $250,000 per depositor, per bank, per ownership category, including interest. Capital One similarly allows deposits far above the insured limit. Never treat a bank’s deposit cap as insured coverage. Use the FDIC-safe planner before funding.
