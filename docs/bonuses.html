Is a bonus worth it? Worked comparisons (computed)

  • Citizens savings bonus at $40,000: $1,800 over 12 months = 4.50% simple — more than any verified HYSA pays in interest alone over a year (Marcus 3.50% = $1,400 on $40k), plus whatever the eligible savings account itself pays (its APY is unverified — F-15 — so don’t count on much: even at the flagged example rates, interest adds only ~$8–$12/yr). Catch: you must open checking + savings the same day by Nov 15, 2026, keep $40k+ every day for 12 months, and citizenship of “new money” matters. If the balance dips, monthly bonuses stop (up to 12 paid).
  • Chase $200 at $10,000 for ~90 days vs. Marcus: bonus $200 + ~$0 interest at 0.01% vs. about $86 interest at Marcus over 3 months. The bonus wins for that quarter — then the money should move, because 0.01% earns $1/year on $10,000 vs. $350 at Marcus. Same logic for TD’s $200 (but TD pays later and can claw back on early closure).
  • Wells Fargo $2,000 Premier: 0.80% on $250,000 for ~90 days sounds strong, but compare keeping that money at 3.50%: ~$2,159 in 3 months at Marcus (hypothetical, APY constant). The bonus only wins if the $250k also sits in a high-rate Wells account (e.g., the 3.25% new-customer Platinum) and the $35/month fee is waived — verify both on the offer page before committing.
  • Direct-deposit bonuses (PNC, Truist, Wells Everyday, Citi $325) don’t require parking a balance — their real cost is moving your paycheck and everyday spending. They pair fine with keeping savings at a HYSA elsewhere.

3-month Marcus interest estimates use Interest = P×[(1+APY)0.25−1] at 3.50% and are hypothetical (variable APY).
