Bottom line (October 22, 2026)

Best verified “no-strings” savings rate at a big national bank: Marcus by Goldman Sachs Online Savings — 3.50% APY (as of Oct 21, 2026), no minimum deposit, no fees, same rate on every dollar. Official Marcus page.

U.S. Bank Smartly Savings also reaches 3.50% APY, but only if you also hold an eligible U.S. Bank product and keep a Combined Qualifying Balance of $25,000+ — without that relationship it pays just 0.05%. American Express and Capital One pay 3.20%, Ally pays 3.10% — all with no minimums and no monthly fees. Traditional branch savings at Chase, Wells Fargo (standard), Truist and TD pays 0.01%–0.04% — on $250,000 that is $25–$100 a year instead of $8,750 at 3.50%. If you can lock money up, big-bank CDs pay more: up to 4.40% APY (Marcus 12-month to 6-year, Ally 18-month, Capital One 11-month special, Amex 10- and 22-month).

This is a research comparison, not personal financial advice. Savings APYs are variable and can change at any time. Always confirm the rate on the bank’s official page with your own ZIP code on the day you open. See full return tables → · How this was verified →

⚠ Three traps this research uncovered

  1. “Up to” rates that most people won’t get. U.S. Bank’s 3.50% needs a linked product + $25k combined balance (otherwise 0.05%–2.00%). Wells Fargo’s 3.25% needs new-customer status + $25k kept every day (otherwise 0.01%). TD’s top Signature rate needs $250,000 plus a linked account — and TD would not show us the number without a location (F-04).
  2. Discover is no longer a separate bank for FDIC purposes. Discover merged into Capital One, N.A. on May 18, 2025. New Capital One + Discover deposits share one $250,000 limit per ownership category — opening both does not double your coverage. (F-10)
  3. Depositing exactly $250,000 overshoots FDIC coverage once interest posts. The limit covers principal plus interest, combined with your other same-category deposits at that bank (checking + savings + CDs). Deposit less, use a joint account ($250k per co-owner), or split across banks. (Planner →)

How “big national bank” was defined

Banks are ranked by the Federal Reserve’s U.S. Domestically Chartered Commercial Banks list (data as of June 30, 2026, posted Oct 2, 2026). This round covers 13 FDIC charters from that list that sell consumer savings to the general public: JPMorgan Chase (#1), Bank of America (#2), Citibank (#3), Wells Fargo (#4), Goldman Sachs/Marcus (#5), U.S. Bank (#6), Capital One incl. Discover (#7), PNC (#8), Truist (#9), TD (#13), Citizens incl. Citizens Access (#19), American Express (#21) and Ally (#23). Custody/wealth-only banks (BNY, State Street, Morgan Stanley, Northern Trust) and the next tier of regionals (Fifth Third, Huntington, BMO, KeyBank, Regions…) are listed as out-of-scope on the Methodology page — no small local banks were included, as requested.
