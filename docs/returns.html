The $250,000 FDIC limit — and why depositing $250,000 overshoots it

FDIC insurance covers $250,000 per depositor, per insured bank, per ownership category — including accrued interest, combining all your deposits in that category at that bank (checking + savings + money market + CDs). Sources: FDIC Deposit Insurance FAQ and Your Insured Deposits. Two consequences:

  • Deposit $250,000 at 3.50% and after one year you hold $258,750 — the last $8,750 is uninsured (if single-category and your only category at that bank).
  • Brands ≠ banks. Capital One and Discover are now one charter (merged May 18, 2025); Marcus = Goldman Sachs Bank USA; Citizens Access = Citizens Bank, N.A. Splitting across brands of the same charter adds no coverage. Splitting across different charters in our league table does.

Ways to stay fully insured with more cash: deposit less (table below), use a joint account ($250,000 per co-owner), add another ownership category (e.g., revocable trust with beneficiaries — rules differ), or use more than one FDIC charter. The FDIC’s EDIE estimator computes your exact coverage.

Will today’s leader stay competitive?

No one can know future variable APYs, and this site does not predict rate paths. What the verified facts do show: (1) the gap between online HYSAs (3.10%–3.50%) and the FDIC average (0.38%) is structural in how these products are priced today — branch standard rates of 0.01% have barely moved across rate cycles at several banks, while HYSAs reprice frequently in both directions; (2) conditional headline rates (U.S. Bank, Wells) can vanish the month you miss a balance test, so their effective competitiveness is lower than the headline suggests; (3) if you need certainty over 1–5 years, a CD at 4.40% locks today’s rate — at the cost of early-withdrawal penalties. Re-check rates a few times a year on the official pages linked throughout this site; switching HYSAs is free at the no-fee banks above.
